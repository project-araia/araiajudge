Judge whether this document is relevant to critical infrastructure — the physical and
operational systems, facilities, networks, and assets that deliver essential services to
society, including their design, development, engineering, construction, operation,
maintenance, planning, regulation, workforce, security, reliability, and resilience.

Sixteen critical infrastructure sectors are in scope. They are listed in full below.

Definitions (as used in this context):
- Critical infrastructure: the physical systems, facilities, networks, and assets that provide an essential service (energy, water, transportation, communications, health, food, finance, government, defense, manufacturing).
- Asset or facility: a built, physical thing that is operated — a plant, station, terminal, treatment works, substation, tower, data center, port, warehouse, hospital building, pipeline segment.
- System or network: an interconnected set of such assets delivering a service (a grid, a water distribution system, a rail network, a cellular network).
- Operations: how a system is run day to day — dispatch, scheduling, control, monitoring, restoration, service delivery, capacity management.
- Design and development: engineering, siting, specification, construction, modernization, upgrade, expansion, or retrofit of infrastructure assets and systems.
- Maintenance and asset management: inspection, condition assessment, repair, replacement, life-cycle management, aging infrastructure.
- Interdependency: reliance of one infrastructure system on another (e.g., water treatment depending on electricity, telecom depending on backup power).
- Resilience: the ability of an infrastructure system to withstand, absorb, restore, recover from, or adapt to a disruption. Psychological, emotional, ecological, and organizational resilience do NOT qualify.
- Disruption: an outage, failure, interruption, degradation, or loss of service in an infrastructure system. A laboratory or biological "disruption" does not qualify.
- Risk: the likelihood or magnitude of harm to an infrastructure system, its service, or its users. Pure financial risk, clinical/medical risk, and abstract statistical risk do NOT qualify.
- Regulation: institutional or policy arrangements governing infrastructure sectors (e.g., utility commissions, safety standards, permitting, reliability standards). Corporate governance, data governance, and AI governance do NOT qualify.

THE CORE TEST — apply this first, before anything else. A document is RELEVANT only if it substantively involves at least ONE of the sixteen critical
infrastructure sectors listed below, AND it does so as a real focus of the work — not
merely as an example, an application, or a passing mention.

Two rules about this test that you must apply literally:

1. **ONE sector is enough.** The document does not need to touch multiple sectors. A paper
   entirely about seaports is relevant. A paper entirely about wastewater treatment plants
   is relevant.
2. **Do not judge relevance from isolated keywords.** A single matching word does not make a
   document relevant. Ask what the document is actually *about*.

If the infrastructure connection is missing, weak, or only mentioned in passing, the document
is NOT relevant (mark `maybe` or `irrelevant`).

THE SIXTEEN SECTORS

Energy — Electricity
  In scope: electric utilities, cooperatives, municipal and investor-owned utilities, public power; power grids, transmission and distribution lines, substations, transformers, switchgear, conductors, poles, undergrounding, smart grids, microgrids, SCADA, advanced metering; power plants and generation (coal, gas, nuclear, hydro, solar, wind, geothermal), battery and grid-scale storage, distributed energy resources; outages, restoration, reliability indices (SAIDI, SAIFI, CAIDI), blackouts, load shedding, dispatch, frequency and voltage stability; resource planning, load forecasting, peak demand, capacity and electricity markets, interconnection; vegetation management, wildfire mitigation plans, public safety power shutoffs; demand response, efficiency, electrification, EV charging; utility commissions, rate cases, NERC and reliability standards.
  Not in scope: battery electrochemistry, solar cell materials, or power-electronics device physics studied as materials or devices rather than as part of a power system.

Energy — Oil and Natural Gas
  In scope: oil and gas production, wells, drilling, offshore platforms, fields and reservoirs; refineries, gas processing plants, LNG terminals and liquefaction, storage (including underground gas storage and the strategic petroleum reserve); gas utilities and local distribution companies; oil and gas pipelines, compressor and pumping stations, pipeline integrity, inspection, corrosion, leaks and ruptures, PHMSA.
  Not in scope: petroleum chemistry, catalysis, or combustion studied purely as chemistry.

Nuclear
  In scope: the full nuclear fuel cycle as physical facilities — nuclear power plants and reactors (including small modular and advanced reactors), reactor safety and containment; uranium conversion, enrichment, and fuel fabrication; reprocessing facilities; spent fuel pools, dry cask and interim storage, geologic repositories, radioactive waste management; nuclear regulation and safeguards, NRC.
  Not in scope: nuclear, particle, or plasma physics as basic science; fusion research (tokamak, magnetic or inertial confinement); radiation biology and medical isotopes.

Dams
  In scope: dams and reservoirs of all kinds — hydroelectric and pumped-storage dams, penstocks, powerhouses; flood-control and water-supply dams, impoundments; dam safety, inspection, failure, breach, rehabilitation, removal, high-hazard dams; levees, floodwalls, floodgates, spillways, reservoir operation and management; navigation locks.
  Not in scope: reservoir engineering in the oil and gas sense; reservoir limnology or fisheries ecology with no dam infrastructure focus.

Water and Wastewater
  In scope: water utilities, public and community water systems, water districts and authorities; drinking water treatment, filtration, disinfection, distribution mains, service lines, pumping stations, storage tanks and towers, intakes, wells; water quality regulation and compliance (Safe Drinking Water Act, maximum contaminant levels, lead service lines, PFAS in drinking water); wastewater and sewer systems, collection, lift stations, treatment works and POTWs, activated sludge, biosolids, effluent, combined and sanitary sewer overflows, water reuse and reclamation; stormwater systems, drainage, storm sewers, culverts, detention basins, green infrastructure; water outages, boil-water advisories, non-revenue water, pipe breaks.
  Not in scope: aquatic ecology, oceanography, fisheries, limnology, and pure hydrology with no engineered water system.

Transportation
  In scope, across all modes:
    Aviation — airports, runways, taxiways, air traffic control and management, airspace, air carriers, air cargo, aircraft operations, FAA.
    Highway and motor carrier — highways, roads, pavement, bridges and bridge condition, tunnels, culverts, interchanges, traffic signals, intelligent transportation systems, trucking and motor carriers, freight corridors, FHWA, FMCSA.
    Maritime — seaports, marine and container terminals, harbors, port authorities and operations, navigation channels, inland and coastal waterways, dredging, locks, vessels, barges, vessel traffic, USCG, MARAD.
    Transit and passenger rail — mass transit and public transportation, transit agencies, bus rapid transit, light rail, streetcar, subway and metro, commuter and high-speed rail, stations, rolling stock, signaling and positive train control, FTA, FRA.
    Freight rail — freight railroads, Class I and short-line railroads, intermodal terminals, rail yards, track and rail corridors, rail bridges and tunnels, locomotives and rail cars, hazmat and crude by rail.
    Pipelines — pipeline networks, operations, integrity, safety, inspection, and incidents, in any commodity. (Pipelines are covered here once; a pipeline paper is relevant regardless of whether it is framed as energy or transportation.)
    Postal and shipping — postal service and facilities, mail processing and sorting, parcel and package delivery, last-mile delivery, courier networks, sortation and fulfillment centers.
  Not in scope: molecular, cellular, ion, membrane, or electron transport; animal migration and wildlife corridors; the traveling-salesman and vehicle-routing problems treated as pure optimization theory.

Communications
  In scope: wireline networks, telephone infrastructure, central offices, local exchange carriers, fiber optic networks and backbone, dark fiber, DSL, VoIP; wireless networks and carriers, cell towers and sites, base stations, radio access networks, small cells, 4G/5G/LTE, spectrum allocation, backhaul, fixed wireless; satellite communications, constellations, ground stations and teleports, uplink and downlink; cable systems and operators, hybrid fiber-coax, DOCSIS, headends; broadcasting — television and radio stations, broadcast towers and transmitters, the emergency alert system; broadband deployment and access, FCC, NTIA.
  Not in scope: animal and interpersonal communication; science, health, and political communication; media studies; pure information theory and channel-capacity results.

Information Technology
  In scope: data centers, colocation facilities, server farms; internet exchange points, internet backbone, tier-1 networks, peering, BGP, DNS and root servers; content delivery networks; cloud infrastructure and service providers, hyperscale facilities; submarine and undersea cables; network operations centers, carrier hotels, ISP infrastructure, edge computing facilities.
  Not in scope: software engineering, programming languages, compilers, algorithms, computational complexity, machine learning methods, and general cybersecurity research that is not about infrastructure systems.

Chemical
  In scope: chemical and petrochemical plants, facilities, and complexes; chemical processing, production, storage, warehousing, and terminals; bulk and hazardous chemical storage and tanks; chemical releases, spills, accidents, and incidents; process safety management, risk management program facilities, CFATS and chemical facility security; transport of hazardous chemicals by pipeline or rail.
  Not in scope: laboratory and synthetic chemistry — reaction mechanisms and kinetics, catalyst design, organic and polymer synthesis, computational and quantum chemistry, spectroscopy, crystallography.

Critical Manufacturing
  In scope: manufacturing and industrial facilities and plants as operating infrastructure — primary metals, steel mills, smelters, foundries; machinery, electrical equipment, and transportation equipment manufacturing; automotive and aerospace assembly plants; shipyards and shipbuilding; semiconductor fabrication facilities and foundries; manufacturing and industrial capacity, supply chains, resilience, reshoring.
  Not in scope: materials science and synthesis — thin films, epitaxy, nanoparticles, nanocomposites, graphene, carbon nanotubes, quantum dots — and semiconductor device physics, as distinct from fabrication facilities as industrial infrastructure.

Commercial Facilities
  In scope: commercial buildings and real estate, office towers, high-rises; shopping malls and retail centers; hotels and lodging; convention centers; stadiums, arenas, entertainment venues, amusement parks, casinos; public assembly and mass-gathering venues — treated as facilities with operations, capacity, safety, or security concerns.
  Not in scope: retail marketing, consumer behavior, tourism demand studies, and real estate as a financial asset class.

Defense Industrial Base
  In scope: defense industrial and manufacturing facilities, defense contractor plants; munitions, ammunition, and ordnance plants; arsenals, military depots, naval shipyards; defense aerospace facilities; defense supply chains, logistics, and industrial capacity.
  Not in scope: weapon system design and effectiveness, military doctrine, combat operations, and warfare strategy.

Emergency Services
  In scope: fire stations and departments, police stations and law enforcement facilities, EMS and ambulance stations; emergency operations centers; public safety answering points, 911 call centers, emergency dispatch; search and rescue and hazmat response capability; public safety communications and first-responder infrastructure.
  Not in scope: clinical emergency medicine and patient triage outcomes.

Financial Services
  In scope: the physical and operational plumbing of finance — financial market infrastructure, payment systems and networks, clearing houses and central counterparties, securities and stock exchanges, ATM and interbank networks, SWIFT, Fedwire, ACH; financial and banking data centers; financial sector operational resilience and cybersecurity.
  Not in scope: asset pricing, portfolio optimization, option and derivative pricing, financial econometrics, market microstructure, behavioral and corporate finance — that is finance research, not infrastructure.

Food and Agriculture
  In scope: food and agricultural processing and handling facilities — food and meat processing plants, poultry and dairy facilities, slaughterhouses; grain elevators and silos, food and cold storage, refrigerated warehouses, distribution centers; livestock facilities and feedlots; agricultural and farm infrastructure; food supply chains and food-system resilience.
  Not in scope: agronomy and crop science, crop yield modeling, plant breeding and genetics, soil microbiology, food chemistry and microbiology, and sensory evaluation.

Government Facilities
  In scope: government buildings and facilities, federal buildings, courthouses, capitols, embassies and consulates; correctional and detention facilities; election infrastructure, polling places, voting systems; school, university, and campus facilities treated as physical infrastructure.
  Not in scope: public administration theory, political science, electoral behavior and voter turnout, and education research on pedagogy or student outcomes.

Healthcare and Public Health
  In scope: hospitals and healthcare facilities as physical infrastructure — buildings, utilities and backup power, evacuation, surge capacity, continuity of operations, facility resilience; long-term care and nursing facilities; public health laboratories and infrastructure; medical, pharmaceutical, vaccine, and blood supply chains; the strategic national stockpile.
  Not in scope: clinical medicine and trials, patient and treatment outcomes, diagnostics, epidemiology, drug development, and biomedical research.

CROSS-CUTTING TOPICS

These qualify when they are about infrastructure systems. They may appear alongside a named
sector or, when clearly infrastructure-framed, on their own:

- interdependency and coupling between infrastructure systems, system-of-systems analysis, lifeline interdependency
- cascading failure, failure and disruption propagation, common-cause failure across infrastructure
- infrastructure resilience, vulnerability, and risk assessment; adaptive and absorptive capacity; all-hazards analysis; disaster preparedness; business continuity and continuity of operations
- infrastructure restoration and recovery, recovery and restoration planning, prioritization, and time to restore
- infrastructure policy, regulation, and governance; sector risk management agencies; the National Infrastructure Protection Plan; critical infrastructure designation
- infrastructure and component supply chains, supply chain disruption and resilience, single points of failure
- asset management, aging infrastructure, condition assessment, deferred maintenance, life-cycle cost, infrastructure renewal and modernization, investment gaps
- cyber-physical security of infrastructure — operational technology and ICS/SCADA security, infrastructure cyberattacks, physical security and sabotage of infrastructure
- the infrastructure and utility workforce, skilled trades, operator training
- federal infrastructure funding and programs — FEMA, hazard mitigation grants and plans, BRIC, disaster declarations, public assistance, IIJA and the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law

THE PHYSICAL / OPERATIONAL BAR — the hardest calls

Relevance requires the *physical or operational system*, not the professional field that
shares its name. Several sectors will otherwise attract large volumes of unrelated research.
Apply these boundaries literally:

- Healthcare: a hospital's backup power, flood exposure, evacuation, or surge capacity is IN. A study of patient outcomes, a clinical trial, or a drug's efficacy is OUT — even though both happen in hospitals.
- Financial services: payment rails, clearing, exchange operations, and financial data centers are IN. Asset pricing, portfolio theory, and market forecasting are OUT.
- Food and agriculture: processing plants, cold chain, grain elevators, and food distribution facilities are IN. Crop yields, plant breeding, and soil science are OUT.
- Chemical: an operating plant, its storage, and its process safety are IN. Bench chemistry that could be done in any laboratory is OUT.
- Information technology: data centers, exchange points, and submarine cables are IN. Software, algorithms, and machine learning methods are OUT.
- Government facilities: the building, the election system as deployed infrastructure, is IN. The politics conducted inside it is OUT.
- Critical manufacturing: the fab, the mill, the shipyard as an operating industrial facility is IN. The material or device being made, studied as a material or device, is OUT.

The general form of this test: **if the document's subject would be unchanged with the
infrastructure removed, the infrastructure is not the subject.**

Decision and score — these **MUST agree**. Choose the score first, then set `decision` from it:

- score 3 -> decision "relevant": Strongly relevant. One or more critical infrastructure sectors, or a cross-cutting infrastructure topic, is the central focus of the document.
- score 2 -> decision "relevant": Relevant. The infrastructure connection is clear and substantive but secondary to a broader topic — for example, a multi-sector study that covers one sector well, a methods or modeling paper whose case study is a real infrastructure system, or a study of infrastructure interdependency in general terms.
- score 1 -> decision "maybe": Partial or indirect. Infrastructure appears but is thin or underdeveloped, OR the document is about a sector's field of study rather than its physical systems. Potentially useful but unclear.
- score 0 -> decision "irrelevant": No meaningful critical infrastructure content.

**Never pair a high score with "irrelevant" or a 0 score with "relevant".**

EXCLUSIONS — mark IRRELEVANT unless the document explicitly centers on infrastructure

By domain:
- Biomedical and clinical: protein structure, gene expression, genomics, transcriptomics, cell signaling, neural circuits, clinical trials, patient outcomes, drug delivery, oncology, tumors, pathogens.
- Laboratory chemistry and materials: reaction kinetics and mechanisms, catalyst design, organic and polymer synthesis, computational and quantum chemistry, NMR, mass spectrometry, crystallography, X-ray diffraction, thin-film deposition, sputtering, epitaxy, nanoparticle synthesis, quantum dots, graphene, carbon nanotubes.
- Astronomy and astrophysics: stellar, galactic, exoplanet, black hole, neutron star, dark matter, dark energy, redshift.
- Particle, high-energy, and fusion physics: hadron, quark, lepton, boson, particle accelerator, collider, tokamak, magnetic and inertial confinement fusion.
- Abstract network and optimization theory: complex network theory, random graphs, scale-free and small-world networks, social network analysis, traveling salesman, vehicle routing — when no real infrastructure system is studied.
- Pure computer science and software: software engineering, compilers, programming languages, computational complexity, machine learning methods, general cybersecurity research.
- Finance and economics theory: asset pricing, portfolio optimization, option and derivative pricing, financial econometrics, behavioral finance.
- Psychology, ecology, and organizational research: psychological and emotional resilience, trauma and addiction recovery, ecological and ecosystem resilience, trophic cascades, species and habitat recovery, organizational resilience theory, human resource management.

By misused word — these words frequently appear with no infrastructure meaning at all:
- "transmission" of disease or signals; "distribution" in statistics; "grid" in imaging, meshing, or numerical methods; "transport" of molecules, ions, or electrons; "network" meaning a social or neural network; "port" in software or biology; "pipeline" meaning a data, computational, or drug-development pipeline; "plant" meaning vegetation; "host" in biology; "cascade" in biochemistry or ecology; "resilience" of a person or an ecosystem; "load" and "stress" in mechanics or physiology; "channel" in electrophysiology.

Important judging guidance — follow these steps in order:

1. Identify what the document is actually about in one sentence, ignoring individual keywords.
2. Ask which sector, if any, that subject belongs to. Name it.
3. Apply the physical/operational bar: is this the physical or operational system, or is it the academic field that shares vocabulary with it?
4. Ask whether the infrastructure is a real focus of the work, or only motivation, background, or an example. Use this test: **remove the infrastructure — does the document still have the same subject?** If yes, the infrastructure is not substantive.
5. Assign the score, then set the decision to match.

Further guidance:

- **When genuinely torn between two scores, pick the HIGHER one.** This screen favors recall: it is better to pass along a borderline infrastructure document than to discard it.
- Judge only from the provided evidence. Sectionized text may omit references and some details; **do not assume content that is not shown.**
- A document can be relevant even if it is about policy, regulation, economics, workforce, equity, or planning rather than engineering, as long as the infrastructure connection is clear.
- A document about a hazard (storm, flood, wildfire, earthquake, heat, cold) is relevant when it connects to an infrastructure system — damage, outage, restoration, hardening, design standards. A hazard study with no infrastructure tie is a 1 at most.
- If the title and abstract are vague but multiple sections substantively discuss infrastructure content, mark relevant.
- If only one weak sentence mentions infrastructure and the rest is unrelated, mark irrelevant.
- State in your rationale which sector or cross-cutting topic you identified, or say explicitly that none was found.

Examples (decision / score / why):

- "Grid Hardening and Restoration Strategies for Distribution Systems After Hurricanes"
  -> relevant / 3. Energy–Electricity. The distribution grid and its restoration are the central subject.
- "Structural Design of Runway Pavement for Heavy Aircraft Loading"
  -> relevant / 3. Transportation–Aviation. Design of a physical infrastructure asset. No hazard or failure is needed for relevance.
- "Condition Assessment and Replacement Prioritization for Aging Water Mains"
  -> relevant / 3. Water. Asset management of a physical distribution system.
- "Seismic Retrofit Options for a Regional Freight Rail Bridge"
  -> relevant / 3. Transportation–Freight Rail. A named asset, its hazard exposure, and its retrofit.
- "Backup Power and Evacuation Planning for Coastal Hospitals"
  -> relevant / 3. Healthcare, on the facility side of the physical/operational bar — power and evacuation, not clinical care.
- "Multi-Sector Infrastructure Interdependencies During Extreme Weather"
  -> relevant / 2. Cross-cutting interdependency plus several named sectors, each covered but sharing the focus.
- "A Graph-Based Framework for Modeling Interdependent Infrastructure Failure"
  -> relevant / 2. Cross-cutting interdependency and cascading failure, clearly infrastructure-framed, though no single sector is central.
- "Machine Learning for Anomaly Detection in Water Treatment SCADA Data"
  -> relevant / 2. Water plus cyber-physical security. A methods paper whose case study is a real operating system.
- "Clinical Outcomes of Sepsis Treatment in Intensive Care Units"
  -> irrelevant / 0. Healthcare vocabulary, but this is clinical medicine. The hospital is the setting, not the subject.
- "Optimal Portfolio Allocation Under Interest Rate Uncertainty"
  -> irrelevant / 0. Finance research, not financial market infrastructure. No payment, clearing, or exchange system appears.
- "Effects of Nitrogen Application Rate on Winter Wheat Grain Yield"
  -> irrelevant / 0. Agronomy. Food and Agriculture is in scope as processing and distribution facilities, not crop science.
- "Galvanostatic Cycling Behavior of a New Lithium-Ion Cathode"
  -> irrelevant / 0. Battery electrochemistry. "Energy storage" as a keyword does not make this a power-system document.
- "Transmission Dynamics of a Foodborne Pathogen in Poultry Flocks"
  -> irrelevant / 0. "Transmission" is epidemiological and "poultry" is not a processing facility. Both matches are vocabulary collisions.
- "Cascade Robustness of Scale-Free Networks Under Random Node Removal"
  -> irrelevant / 0. Abstract network theory. "Cascading failure" language appears, but no real infrastructure system is studied.
- "Drought Impacts on Reservoir Ecosystems in the Southwest"
  -> maybe / 1. A real hazard and a real reservoir, but the subject is ecology, with no dam, water supply, or hydropower system examined.
- "National Energy Outlook: Primary Fuel Mix and Emissions Projections"
  -> maybe / 1. Energy in the broad sense, with only an indirect link to physical generation, delivery, or utility systems.
